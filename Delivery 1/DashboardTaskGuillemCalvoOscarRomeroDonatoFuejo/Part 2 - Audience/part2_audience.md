# Part 2. Audience

Group 3

## Target audience and reporting context

We designed the dashboard for analysts at a tourism observatory who prepare a monthly report on Lisbon and the Algarve. They already use line charts, bar charts and percentages in their weekly work, so we chose familiar charts they could read together on an office desktop. Our bookings come from one city hotel in Lisbon and one resort in the Algarve. That gives the analysts two places to compare, but they would need records from more hotels before drawing conclusions about either region.

## Analyst persona and reporting workflow

Natalia, 47, is a tourism analyst with a degree in economics. She advises the regional tourism board on when and where to promote Lisbon and the Algarve. To plan a campaign, she wants to know when the city hotel and the resort are busiest, which countries their guests come from and how far in advance those guests tend to book.

In the first few days of each month, Natalia sits down at her desk to prepare her report. She opens the dashboard and reads the key figures, then follows the two hotels through the seasons to see where their busiest months coincide and where they differ. Natalia has deuteranopia, a form of red-green colour vision deficiency, so the labels and different line styles help her keep track of each hotel as she reads. Once she has a sense of the seasonal pattern, she turns to the detail screen to see which markets account for it. She uses what she finds to recommend whom the next campaign should reach and when it should begin.

## Analytical questions by dashboard screen

| # | Question | Screen |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | When does each hotel receive the most guests? | Main |
| Q2 | Where do guests come from, and how does the mix differ between hotels? | Detail |
| Q3 | When do guests from each market travel? | Detail |
| Q4 | How long do guests from each market stay? | Detail |

| Q6 | How far ahead do guests from each market book? | Detail |
