The audience: who we design for

We are designing the dashboard for Jaume Collboni, Mayor of Barcelona, or a member of his road-safety team. He knows city policy well but is not a statistician, so every chart must be readable and understood inmediately. He will use it on a tablet in landscape orientation, about once a week and before council reunions.

In a typical scenario, he prepares for a council debate on road safety. He opens the dashboard to learn whether the city is getting safer, where to intervene and when. Our dataset (2019-2025) holds a key warning: recorded accidents fell 23% between 2019 and 2025 (10,027 to 7,741), while seriously injured people rose 23% (202 to 249). He must not confuse fewer accidents with safer outcomes.

The dashboard therefore answers seven questions: how accidents and serious injuries evolve (trends, line charts); which districts have the most accidents and the highest serious share (comparison, choropleth and sorted bars); when accidents concentrate (patterns, time-based heatmap); which weekdays are most severe (comparison, bars); which pedestrian behaviours cause pedestrian accidents (composition, sorted bars); where hotspots are (location, point map); and how reliable the data are (quality, annotated notes). Each question is matched to the chart type that best serves its function, keeping the design simple, annotated and friendly for a non-expert audience.
